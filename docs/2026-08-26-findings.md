# What held up — 26 August 2026

Every test below was registered and committed **before** it ran, so each registration commit is an
ancestor of its own results commit and "we registered this first" is checkable rather than asserted.

11 underlyings · 30 months of inverted implied volatility · 24 commits.

---

## The premise holds

Rank names by how rich their implied volatility is against their **own** history, and the top of that
ranking retains more of the premium it sells.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rank IC** | **+0.1753** — IV percentile vs. premium retained, 1,807 name-days |
| **Control arm** | **−0.1055** — same test on *raw* IV level |
| **Permutation p** | **0.0010** — beat all 1,000 within-day shuffles, seed 42 |

**The control is the finding.** A positive IC alone proves little, because implied volatility appears
on both sides of a naive test. Ranking on the raw *level* comes back **negative** — selling whatever
has the highest absolute IV is mildly harmful. The edge lives specifically in the comparison to a
name's own history.

## And it isn't an earnings detector

The strongest outside objection was that "this name's IV is unusually high" mostly means "earnings are
coming" — in which case filtering earnings out wouldn't protect the strategy, it would delete it.

| Sample | Name-days | Rank IC | Perm p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All name-days | 1,807 | **+0.1753** | 0.0010 |
| ±2 sessions around events removed | 1,674 | **+0.1831** | 0.0010 |
| Forward window entirely event-free | 1,145 | **+0.1561** | 0.0010 |

The first exclusion reached only **7.4%** of the contamination — the outcome measures volatility over
the following 21 sessions, so an announcement lands inside it even when the entry sits weeks away. The
powered test removes **36.6%** of the sample and the result holds. Earnings dates came from SEC 8-K
Item 2.02 filings, where the filing date *is* the announcement date.

Six behaviours measured by direct call. Four contradict the documentation, which is the reason for
measuring rather than reading.

| Behaviour | Measured | |
|---|---|---|
| Implied volatility and greeks | Absent from an otherwise-200 response — OPRA-gated. IV must be **computed** by inversion, never read | docs wrong |

| Historical bars including today | **403** for any window ending today; 200 ending yesterday, with data right up to it | undocumented |
| Options quote feed latency | 1.43s / −0.06s / 0.11s — **real-time**, not the documented 15-minute delay | docs wrong |
| Earnings calendar | Not among the 15 corporate-action types. There is no upstream to ingest | absent |

## Execution costs what the book charges, not what the spec says

Two live round trips, NBBO captured at every submission because it cannot be reconstructed afterwards.

| Underlying | Leg spreads | Limit at mid | Execution vs mid |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| SPY | 3¢ | Filled better than mid, 127 ms | **+0.015** |
| AMD | 14–66¢ | Rested 26 s, never filled | **−0.310** |

Fills clear better than the touch by a small **absolute** amount that does not scale with spread
width — so execution *earns* on a tight book and *costs* on a wide one. A cost model fitted on SPY
alone would be wrong for every other name. Fees resolved exactly: **$0.025 per contract-leg**,
identical residual across trades differing 9× in notional.

## Five things that did not survive

All five were caught by self-audit after the run, not by review. Two would have shipped a headline
number that meant nothing.

**Claimed:** execution beats a bid/ask backtest by 0.165 — 27% of premium.
**Actually:** that subtracted a cost from a P&L already containing a market move. Like-for-like:
0.100. Then superseded entirely when a second round trip showed the result was specific to SPY.

**Claimed:** the signal's information coefficient is 0.16.
**Actually:** the outcome and the signal were both functions of the same implied-volatility term, so
the statistic measured itself. Voided and re-registered with a scale-free outcome and a control arm.

**Claimed:** a measurement-noise ratio makes a sound eligibility filter.
**Actually:** it was registered as a diagnostic, not a gate, and applying it left a **one-name**
universe. Tested properly, the choice of price print moves the ranking by 2–5 percentile points
against a noise level of 29.

**Claimed:** historical spreads cannot be estimated — the best proxy correlates +0.036 across names.
**Actually:** premature. Only bar statistics had been tried. Roll's estimator on trade sequences
reaches **+0.500**, moving the cost model from unfittable to estimable.

**Claimed:** option bars lag five trading days.
**Actually:** a misread flag. `limit` truncates from the start of the window, so the single returned
bar was the oldest, not the newest.

## What is not established

Stated as plainly as the results, because a page that only lists what worked is advertising.

- **No backtested return is quotable.** The spread estimator is not fitted, and one calibrated on a
  single session is not a calibration.
- The structure and strike selection have **never been reviewed** — an adversarial review was in
  flight when this was written.
- The scheduled cycle has **never placed an order**; both round trips were placed by hand.
- Universe chosen on today's liquidity, applied to 2024–25 data — **survivorship**, disclosed.
- A 30-day reference tenor stands in for a 5–9 day traded structure. A deliberate substitution,
  never tested.

## Method

Three registrations still failed today — not on their thresholds, but on the structure around them:
a decision table that omitted the outcome that occurred, twice, and a statistic that shared a term
with its own outcome. The thresholds got the care; the scaffolding did not.
